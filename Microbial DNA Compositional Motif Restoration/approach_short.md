Since train and test come from different genomes, I split my validation by genome so I was always testing on unseen ones. My first model was based on k-mer co-occurrence, but it scored below the baseline. Looking closer, I saw the masked token was already in one of the sibling contigs about 94% of the time, so the issue was ranking it highly, not finding it. What worked was collaborative filtering: for each contig I rank the candidate tokens by how often they show up in the most similar sibling contigs, giving more weight to rarer shared k-mers and less to common ones. I also tried a small transformer, but it overfit the genomes, so I went with the simpler count-based approach.
