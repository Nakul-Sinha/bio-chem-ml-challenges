The first thing I did was line up the train and test notes side by side, and they looked nothing alike: training notes were clean and fully labeled, but test notes were unlabeled, showed only three of the six operations, and used bench-tag suffixes I'd never seen before. I realized the real signal lives in each tag's word-prefix, not the digits. So I trained on degraded copies of every training row that imitate the messy test format — that's what teaches the model to read the tags, apply the correction notice, and infer the missing slots itself.

For the model, instead of generating the six-slot string left-to-right I put a T5 encoder under six small classifier heads, one per slot. Generating left-to-right kept collapsing the last slot (workup); predicting all six at once fixed that and also did better on the two heavily-weighted slots, control and quench. Then I average a handful of these models trained from different seeds. That took my cross-validation from about 0.726 up to 0.736, and it still fits the 30-minute A10G budget because it times itself and stops after however many seeds fit.
